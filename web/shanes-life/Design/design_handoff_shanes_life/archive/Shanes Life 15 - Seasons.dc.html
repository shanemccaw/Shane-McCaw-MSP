The year, in the house

The critters dress for the date. Four seasons underneath, five holidays on top; it changes on its own, by calendar and by clock. Nothing to set. The first phone is computed from today's real date, the rest are the holidays and the other seasons.

The calendar

  • Fall, Sep to Nov. Leaves drift down the screen.
  • October is Halloween. Witch hat on the fox, a ghost peeks over Next, bats cross the sky, jack-o'-lanterns glow in the yard, a spider under the eave.
  • Oct 25 to 31 is birthday week. Bunting and balloons go up on the house while Halloween carries on. Oct 28 is the day: party hats, confetti, a cake in the yard, and the fox says so.
  • Nov 1 to Thanksgiving. Leaf crown, pie steaming in the yard, a turkey waddling past the house.
  • Day after Thanksgiving to Dec 25. Santa hats, string lights twinkling on the roof, snow on the ridge, a wreath, a snowman and a small tree. The raccoon flies a sleigh across the sky behind two reindeer.
  • Dec 26 to Jan 1. New Year: fireworks over the house, party hats, the lights stay up.
  • Winter, Spring, Summer. Snow and a white roof; petals and tulips; fireflies and a sunflower.

Rules

  • One theme at a time. A holiday wins over its season.
  • Hats go on the fox and the peeker only. Room critters stay themselves, so nothing gets hard to read.
  • Motion stays quiet: drift, sway, flicker, twinkle. Fireworks are the one loud thing, and they get a week.
  • Everything is a flag on a theme (hat, peeker, sky, weather, yard, roof). Adding a holiday is one row, not a redesign. Valentine's, the Fourth, Easter can join later.
  • Time of day still runs underneath: dawn, day, dusk and night skies tint the holiday.
  • The corner light is the weather. Top right, where the nudge count was: temperature and sky for your location, and the glow behind it changes with it. Sun, moon, cloud, rain, snow, and a storm cloud that flashes.
