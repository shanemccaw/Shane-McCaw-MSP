### What shipped
- `desktop/BuildConsole/Controls/KeyedSlotCardHost.cs` (new): keyed, diff-based slot cards. An unchanged slot keeps its card and its already-running pulse; only a changed slot is rebuilt. `BeginOwnedAnimation` starts a pulse through a clock the host keeps, so it can `Controller.Stop()` it on discard.
- `RenderMatrixDrawer()` keys each slot on everything `MatrixSlotCard` displays, and retires the cards (stopping their clocks) while the drawer is collapsed. The collapsed early-return (option 3) already existed before this build.
- `scripts/probes/matrix-pulse-leak/` (kept diagnostic): standalone probe process, never launches BuildConsole; compiles the real `KeyedSlotCardHost.cs`.

### The issue's option 1 was wrong, and measurement caught it
`BeginAnimation(dp, null)` does **not** stop a clock — it only unhooks the element. Probe `--mode detach-experiment`, 400 Forever animations per phase, live clocks measured as pulse invalidations / reference-clock frames: attached 400; `Children.Clear()` only 400 (still ticking); `BeginAnimation(null)` + discard ~361 (falls only when a gen2 GC lands in the window); after a full GC 0; `Controller.Stop()` ~1.8; `KeyedSlotCardHost.Retire()` ~1.8. WPF holds these clocks weakly, so the pile is bounded by GC rather than unbounded as the issue body claimed — but a discarded card keeps doing per-frame work until a collection reclaims it.

### Honest gaps
- Whether this leak alone caused the reported crash is not proven. The probe shows the old pattern settling at ~30-50 ticking clocks rather than growing without bound; how large the pile gets inside the real app, with its bigger heap and rarer gen2 collections, was not measured.
