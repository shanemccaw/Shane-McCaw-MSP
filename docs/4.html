Executive Summary
This tenant has three compounding governance failures operating simultaneously: a quarter of its groups have no owner, no automated lifecycle policy exists to retire workspaces that are no longer needed, and no access reviews are scheduled to periodically confirm that members and guests still belong. These are not isolated gaps — they reinforce each other. A group with no owner cannot be reviewed; a workspace with no expiry never surfaces for recertification; guest accounts invited under an unrestricted policy accumulate without a mechanism to remove them. Seventeen of the 18 Teams are private and all 18 carry both a meeting and a messaging policy, which is a genuine strength, but it is undermined by the fact that the groups backing those workspaces include some with no accountable owner. The Microsoft 365 Message Center holds 440 items, of which 108 are flagged as major changes — a volume that signals the tenant has not established a routine for triaging and acting on platform announcements. The result is a tenant that has provisioned capability without the operational discipline to govern it.
Domain — Ownership & Accountability
NO-GO
26 of 104 groups have no owner
26 groups — exactly one quarter of the tenant's 104 groups — have no assigned owner. The affected groups include workspaces with names that suggest active operational use: Finance, Payroll, Billing, Sales, Legal, Human Solutions, Managed Services, and others. A group without an owner has no one responsible for approving membership requests, reviewing access, or deciding when the group should be retired. It cannot be included in an access review because there is no one to review it. This is the single most consequential governance gap in this tenant because it blocks remediation of nearly every other issue — you cannot govern what no one owns.

CONDITIONAL
All 104 groups are maintained by hand — no dynamic membership rules exist
None of the 104 groups uses a dynamic membership rule to automatically add or remove members based on user attributes such as department, job title, or location. Every group is managed manually. Manual management is not inherently wrong, but at this scale it places the full burden of membership accuracy on individual owners — the same owners who are absent from 26 of those groups. Where an owner leaves or is never assigned, membership drift is guaranteed. Dynamic membership eliminates that category of risk for attribute-driven groups and reduces the operational load on owners for the remainder.
Domain — Lifecycle, Expiry & Sprawl
NO-GO
No group expiration policy is configured — workspaces accumulate indefinitely
The tenant has no group expiration policy. Microsoft 365 groups — and the Teams, SharePoint sites, and shared mailboxes connected to them — will never prompt an owner to confirm the group is still needed. Groups created for a project, a trial, a client engagement, or a test will remain permanently unless someone manually deletes them. Looking at the group names present in this tenant — Trial, test12345, Test NASA, License Test -- Shane, SMC — audit-fresh-1783879442@newclient-test.com — it is clear that test and trial workspaces have already accumulated. Without expiry enforcement, this inventory grows in one direction only.

Domain — External Access & Guest Governance
NO-GO
Any licensed user in the tenant can invite external guests — no restriction is configured
The tenant's B2B collaboration settings are set to allow guest invitations from everyone, with no restriction. This means any licensed internal user can invite an external account into the tenant's Microsoft 365 environment without requiring approval from an administrator or group owner. Combined with the absence of access reviews (see below), this creates an unbounded inflow of guest accounts with no offsetting outflow mechanism. There are currently 24 guest accounts in the tenant, and no process exists to periodically confirm they still require access.
NO-GO
No access review is scheduled for any of the 24 guest accounts
Zero access reviews exist for guest accounts. The tenant has 24 external guest accounts, and the last sign-in date is unavailable for all 24 — the sign-in activity field returned no data for any of them. That absence means it is not possible to determine from this scan which guests are active and which are dormant. With no review scheduled, no guest is ever systematically removed. This is a direct consequence of the open invitation policy: guests arrive without approval and stay without review.
CONDITIONAL
All 18 Teams have guests — and cross-tenant access is configured at the tenant level
Every one of the 18 Teams has guest members. Cross-tenant access settings are configured at the tenant level, which indicates deliberate external collaboration has been set up. That configuration is not itself a problem — it is the absence of the governance controls around it that creates risk. Guest membership in Teams should be reviewed periodically by each team's owner. Because no access review process exists, that review is not happening. The per-team guest settings data was returned by the scan but at a level of detail that does not allow individual team guest settings to be compared in this report.
NOTE
No access reviews are overdue — because none have ever been created
The overdue access review count is zero. This is not a passing grade — it reflects the fact that no access reviews have been configured at all for this tenant. There is nothing to be overdue. Zero reviews scheduled and zero reviews overdue is a worse position than having reviews that are occasionally late.

Notable example titles visible in the scan include retirements of Exchange Web Services, SharePoint legacy features, and OneDrive sync on older Windows builds — changes with hard deadlines attached.

Required & Recommended Actions
Priority Action What It Changes
1 — Immediate
Assign owners to all 26 ownerless groups
Prioritise Break-Glass Accounts - CA Exclusion, Finance, Payroll, Billing, Legal, and Sales. Use the Entra admin center to assign at least two owners per group.
Establishes accountability for one quarter of the tenant's groups. Without this, access reviews, expiry notifications, and membership changes have no one to receive them.
2 — Immediate
Restrict who can invite guest users
Change the B2B collaboration invitation setting from "everyone" to "admins and users in the guest inviter role" or "admins only," depending on the tenant's collaboration model.
Stops uncontrolled external account creation. Every guest added since this setting was configured as open arrived without an approval step.
3 — Immediate
Create access reviews for all 24 guest accounts
Configure a recurring access review in Entra Identity Governance, scoped to guest users, with each group owner as the reviewer. Set a 90-day or 180-day cadence. Enable automatic removal on no response.
Creates the only mechanism by which guests who no longer need access will be removed. Without a review, no guest is ever systematically offboarded.
4 — Short-term
Configure a Microsoft 365 group expiration policy
Set a group lifetime (180 or 365 days is typical) with automatic renewal on activity. Configure the expiry notification to go to group owners 30 days before expiry. Apply to all groups initially, then exempt known permanent groups as needed.
Creates the only mechanism that can shrink the current group estate. Test and trial groups (test12345, Trial, SMC — audit-fresh-1783879442@newclient-test.com) will surface for deletion on their first renewal cycle.
5 — Short-term
Triage the 108 "Plan for Change" and "Prevent or Fix Issue" Message Center items
Work through the "Prevent or fix issue" items first — approximately 12 — as these carry the highest consequence. Then review the "Plan for change" items for hard retirement deadlines. Establish a weekly or fortnightly Message Center review cadence going forward.
Reduces the risk of a service disruption or unplanned feature loss from a retirement that passed its deadline unnoticed. Several announced retirements have configuration or migration steps attached.
6 — Short-term
Establish a group naming policy
Define and enforce a naming convention (e.g. a prefix indicating the workspace type: CLIENT-, DEPT-, MSP-, PROJ-). Apply the policy in Entra and use it as part of the review of the existing 104 groups to clean up test and email-derived names.
Makes purpose and ownership visible in the group name itself, which speeds up access reviews, expiry decisions, and owner assignment.
7 — Ongoing
Evaluate dynamic membership rules for department-aligned groups
For groups that map to HR-defined attributes (department, job title, office location), convert to dynamic membership. This eliminates the class of membership drift caused by manual updates not being made when employees change roles or leave.
Removes manual membership maintenance for attribute-aligned groups, reducing owner burden and eliminating a category of stale membership. Currently, all 104 groups require manual updates.
