Current State

Every one of the tenant's 18 Teams has no designated owner. Twenty-six of 104 Microsoft 365 groups are also ownerless, leaving a quarter of all collaborative workspaces with no accountable administrator. There is no group expiry policy in place — groups and Teams accumulate indefinitely with no automated review trigger. Guest invitations are open to all licensed users in the tenant, 24 guest accounts are present with no access review scheduled, and cross-tenant access is configured but not paired with any recurring review. On the positive side, all 99 SharePoint sites carry a sensitivity label, no stale accounts were detected, no OneDrive transfers are pending for departed users, and all 18 Teams have meeting and messaging policies assigned.

The dominant risk is ownership: when nobody is accountable for a workspace, there is no one to approve guest additions, notice channel sprawl, or act on a policy violation. The fixes below address that structural gap first, then the lifecycle and external-access controls that depend on it.

Ownership & Accountability
NO-GO
All 18 Teams have no owner assigned

Every Team in the tenant — ATRIO, Small Parts Manufacturing, Roofing CRM, Netropole, TARR, Terminal Transfer, TCMS, Template, LifeWorksNW, Hunter Davisson, Vigilant, Sales, Columbia River Carbonates, Barricade MSP, NASA, Test NASA, Finance, and Shane McCaw Consulting - Templates — returns zero owners. With no owner, there is no one to approve or remove members, gate guest additions, archive the team when it becomes inactive, or act when Microsoft notifies of a required policy change. Every governance control that depends on owner-driven approval is inoperative for the entire Teams estate.

NO-GO
26 of 104 Microsoft 365 groups have no owner

One in four groups in the tenant is ownerless. The 26 affected groups include Finance, CRM, Bills, Payroll, Billing, Orders, Sales, Legal, Tax, Resumes, Support, Projects, and others. An ownerless group has no one to authorise membership changes, respond to expiry notifications, or manage the group's associated SharePoint site or shared mailbox. If an expiry policy were enabled today — which it is not — ownerless groups would be deleted automatically because there is no owner to receive and act on the renewal prompt.

The remaining 78 groups do have at least one owner on record. No further breakdown of owner counts per group was available in the scan data.

CONDITIONAL
All 104 groups are maintained by hand — no dynamic membership rules exist

Not one of the 104 groups uses a dynamic membership rule tied to user attributes such as department or job title. Every group is managed manually. For a tenant of this size, manual management is not inherently wrong, but it means membership accuracy depends entirely on administrators remembering to add and remove members when roles change. Combined with the ownership gaps above, there is no automated backstop to catch stale memberships.

Lifecycle, Sprawl & Retirement
NO-GO
No group expiry policy is configured

The tenant has no group expiry policy of any kind. Groups and Teams, once created, persist indefinitely unless an administrator manually deletes them. There is no automated renewal prompt, no expiry notification sent to owners, and no system-driven retirement path. This is how the tenant arrives at 104 groups — many of which, given the naming patterns visible (test12345, Trial, Test NASA, and multiple duplicated names such as two entries each for "TARR," "Break-Glass Accounts - CA Exclusion," "All Company," "SMC — Shane McCaw," and others), appear to include abandoned or duplicated workspaces that have simply never been cleaned up.

CONDITIONAL
27 channels across the Teams estate include test and throwaway channels

The tenant's 18 Teams have a combined total of 27 channels. Several of these carry names that indicate they were created for testing and never removed: "Test," "Test 123," "Test 12345," and "Test" appear as distinct channels. Others — "SharePoint Upgrade," "SharePoint Advanced Search," "Lookup System," "CFS System," "Storage," and "Intranet - HR Compliance" — describe specific projects or systems that may no longer be active. With no owners assigned to any Team and no expiry mechanism, these channels accumulate without review.

CONDITIONAL
One of 18 Teams is set to Public visibility — "Test NASA"

"Test NASA" is the only Team with public visibility, meaning any user in the tenant can join it without approval. Its name identifies it as a test workspace. It has no owner. A public team with a test-phase name and no owner to manage it or its contents is a governance gap — any tenant user can join and access whatever is stored there.

Sharing & External Access
NO-GO
Guest invitations are open to everyone — any licensed user can invite external guests

Both the invitation permission and the guest invite restriction are set to "Everyone." Any licensed user in the tenant can invite an external guest with no administrator approval required. Given that 18 Teams have guests and no team has an owner to govern those guests, the combination means external users can be added to workspaces with no internal accountable party. This is a structural external-access risk, not a theoretical one.

NO-GO
24 guest accounts in the tenant with no access review scheduled

The tenant holds 24 guest accounts. No recurring guest access review has been configured — not a single review definition exists. There is therefore no periodic check to confirm that these external users still need access, that their organisations still hold valid contracts, or that they have not been added in error. All 18 Teams that have guests are in this unreviewed state.

Sign-in activity data was not available for the 24 guest accounts, so it is not possible to determine from the scan how recently any guest last accessed the tenant. The absence of a review schedule means this gap will persist and grow as new guests are invited.

PASS
Cross-tenant access policy is configured

A cross-tenant access policy is in place. This is a structural control that governs how identities from other Entra tenants can authenticate into this one, and its presence is a positive signal. The scan confirmed the policy definition exists; it does not report on the specific rules within it. Maintaining and periodically reviewing this policy is important as guest-access patterns change.

Access Reviews & Account Hygiene
NO-GO
No guest access review is defined in the tenant

Microsoft Entra ID Governance access reviews for guest accounts are not configured. Zero review definitions were found. The 24 guest accounts in the tenant have never been subjected to a structured review cycle, and there is no mechanism to trigger one automatically. This finding, combined with unrestricted guest invitation permissions, means external access accumulates without any formal attestation process.

PASS
No access reviews are currently overdue

Zero overdue access reviews were found. This is a consequence of having no reviews defined at all — it is not evidence that the tenant's access posture is healthy. Nothing can be overdue if nothing has been scheduled.

PASS
No stale internal accounts detected

The scan evaluated 24 accounts for stale sign-in activity and returned zero stale accounts. Internal account hygiene is clean based on the available data. Sign-in activity timestamps were not returned for those accounts, so the conclusion rests on the platform's own staleness classification.

PASS
No OneDrive transfers are pending for departed users

Of the 24 accounts evaluated, none has a OneDrive content transfer pending. Departed user content is not accumulating in unowned drives awaiting reassignment.

Sensitivity Label Adoption & Site Coverage
PASS
All 99 SharePoint sites carry a sensitivity label

Every site in the evaluated SharePoint estate — 99 sites — has a sensitivity label applied. This is complete coverage and is a strong governance signal: each site has been classified, which is the prerequisite for label-driven access controls and data-protection policies to operate correctly. This result stands in contrast to the ownership gaps; the classification layer is in place even where accountable owners are not.

Teams Policy Enforcement
PASS
All 18 Teams have both a meeting policy and a messaging policy assigned

Meeting policy coverage is 18 of 18; messaging policy coverage is 18 of 18. The administrative policy layer is fully applied across the Teams estate. This means tenant-wide controls for meetings and messaging are active on every Team, regardless of owner assignment. Policy enforcement at the administrative level is intact even where workspace-level accountability is absent.

Service Change Awareness
CONDITIONAL
440 Message Center items include 108 flagged as major changes requiring review

The Message Center holds 440 items in total. Of these, 108 are categorised as "Plan for change" — Microsoft's designation for announcements that require the tenant to take deliberate action or prepare before a deadline. A further set are categorised as "Prevent or fix issue," which Microsoft uses for items where inaction causes a service problem or compliance gap. The remainder are "Stay informed" notifications.

108 "Plan for change" items represent a substantial backlog. Among the most operationally significant are the retirement of Exchange Web Services (multiple notices), retirement of legacy whiteboard content moving to OneDrive-backed storage, retirement of SharePoint Designer 2013, retirement of Remote Event Receivers in SharePoint Online, required EWS allow-list updates for calendar access, enforcement of Entra Self-Service Password Reset registered-method requirements from November 2026, and the requirement to update Teams Rooms apps to maintain PowerPoint Live functionality. Each "Plan for change" item carries its own deadline; the scan does not record whether any of these have been acknowledged or actioned by the tenant.

Required & Recommended Actions

Ordered by what each action actually fixes, not by effort or preference.

# Action What it fixes Severity
1 Assign at least one owner to every Team. All 18 Teams — including ATRIO, Roofing CRM, Finance, Sales, NASA, and the remaining 13 — currently have zero owners. Establishes accountability required for every downstream governance control: guest approval, archive decisions, policy notifications. Critical
2 Assign owners to the 26 ownerless Microsoft 365 groups. Prioritise groups with active SharePoint sites or shared mailboxes: Finance, CRM, Payroll, Billing, Orders, Sales, Legal, Tax, Support. Prevents these groups from being auto-deleted if an expiry policy is later enabled, and gives each group an accountable manager. Critical
3 Configure a recurring guest access review in Microsoft Entra ID Governance covering all 24 guest accounts, with a review frequency of at least every 90 days and auto-removal of guests who are not approved. Closes the complete absence of any formal attestation cycle for external users. Without this, guest accounts accumulate indefinitely with no accountability check. Critical
4 Restrict guest invitation permissions from "Everyone" to "Admins and users in specific guest inviter roles." Remove the ability for all licensed users to invite external guests without approval. Stops ungoverned external invitations. Currently any licensed user can add a guest to any workspace, including the 18 Teams that have no owner to manage those guests. Critical
5 Enable a group expiry policy. Set a renewal interval — 180 or 365 days is common — and ensure every group has an owner before the policy is activated, so renewal notifications have a recipient. Creates a systematic retirement path for inactive or abandoned groups and Teams. Currently groups accumulate forever with no automated review trigger. High
6 Review and either archive or delete the public "Test NASA" Team. If retained, convert its visibility to Private and assign an owner. Removes open-join access for all tenant users to an ownerless, purpose-unknown workspace. High
7 Triage the 108 "Plan for change" items in the Message Center. Identify each item that carries a hard Microsoft deadline, assign an owner to act on it, and confirm which of the retirement notices (Exchange Web Services, SharePoint Designer 2013, Remote Event Receivers, legacy whiteboards) affect active integrations or workflows in this tenant. Prevents service disruptions from missed Microsoft-imposed retirement deadlines. Several items have already been issued as reminders, indicating their original deadlines have passed or are approaching. High
8 Remove or archive test and throwaway channels: "Test," "Test 123," "Test 12345" within their respective Teams, and confirm whether project-specific channels ("SharePoint Upgrade," "CFS System," "Lookup System") are still active. Reduces channel sprawl and eliminates dead-end workspaces that add noise for members and dilute discoverability of active collaboration. Medium
