Executive Summary

This tenant has active, meaningful governance gaps in two areas that create concrete risk today: ownership accountability and external-access control. Every one of the 18 Teams in the tenant has no owner on record. Of the 104 groups in the directory, 26 are also ownerless. No group expiration policy is configured, so workspaces accumulate indefinitely with no automated review or retirement gate. Guest invitations are open to all licensed users with no restriction, and the 24 guest accounts in the tenant have never been through a formal access review.

On the positive side, sensitivity label coverage across SharePoint is complete — all 99 sites carry a label. All 18 Teams have both a meeting policy and a messaging policy assigned. No stale member accounts were detected, and none of the 24 guest accounts are flagged as inactive. No OneDrive data from departed users is pending transfer. Cross-tenant access policy is configured.

The CONDITIONAL rating reflects that the tenant's classification and policy-assignment infrastructure is sound, but the accountability and lifecycle controls that make those policies enforceable are absent. The guest-access and group membership management posture leaves the tenant exposed to unreviewed external access. The issues below are ordered by the scope of impact each fix delivers.

Ownership & Accountability
NO-GO Every Team in the tenant has no owner assigned

All 18 Teams — including ATRIO, Roofing CRM, NASA, Finance, Columbia River Carbonates, and Shane McCaw Consulting - Templates — have an ownerless count of zero owners. The full list: ATRIO, Small Parts Manufacturing, Roofing CRM, Netropole, TARR, Terminal Transfer, TCMS, Template, LifeWorksNW, Hunter Davisson, Vigilant, Sales, Columbia River Carbonates, Barricade MSP, NASA, Test NASA, Finance, and Shane McCaw Consulting - Templates.

Without an owner, no one is accountable for membership, channel configuration, or guest access within each Team. Policy enforcement depends on owner-level controls that cannot be exercised. If a member leaves or a project ends, there is no responsible party to initiate archival or deletion. This affects all 18 Teams simultaneously.

NO-GO 26 of 104 directory groups have no owner

Of the 104 groups in the directory, 26 have no owner on record. Named examples from the data include: Finance, AdminAgents, CRM, Bills, QuickBooks, Billing, Payroll, Help-Desk, Orders, Info, Banking, Sales, HelpdeskAgents, Legal, Tax, Resumes, Support, Projects, All Company, Contact, and others. These span functional groups (Finance, Payroll, Legal, Help-Desk) as well as customer-facing groups (Roofing CRM, CRM) and security-adjacent groups (Break-Glass Accounts - CA Exclusion, PS-Execution-DLP-Access).

Groups without owners cannot be reviewed, modified, or decommissioned by a responsible party. Groups like Break-Glass Accounts - CA Exclusion and PS-Execution-DLP-Access carry elevated access implications — having no owner on those groups is a direct governance gap with a security consequence.

CONDITIONAL All 104 groups are maintained by hand — no dynamic membership rules exist

None of the 104 groups in the tenant uses a dynamic membership rule. Every group's membership is maintained manually. This is not a finding that requires immediate remediation in isolation, but combined with the ownerless-group problem above, it means stale or incorrect membership has no automated correction path.

For groups tied to job functions — Finance, Payroll, Sales, Help-Desk — dynamic rules driven by Entra ID attributes (department, job title) would keep membership current as staff change without requiring an owner to act. Without an owner and without dynamic rules, no one is maintaining these groups.

Lifecycle — Creation, Review & Retirement
NO-GO No group expiration policy is configured

The tenant has no group expiration policy. Groups and Teams are created but never automatically reviewed for relevance or sunset. The 104 groups in the directory accumulate indefinitely, with no system-enforced prompt to owners to confirm that a workspace is still needed.

Groups like "test12345", "Test NASA", "Trial", "Template", and "License Test -- Shane" in the directory strongly suggest workspaces created for temporary purposes that were never cleaned up. Without an expiration policy, these persist and carry whatever access permissions were set at creation. A group expiration policy surfaces these to owners (or deletes them automatically when no owner confirms need) on a regular cadence.

CONDITIONAL 27 channels identified, including test and unstructured channels with no clear purpose

Across the 18 Teams, 27 channels were inventoried. Many are named "General" (the default), which is expected, but the list also includes: "Test", "Test 123", "Test 12345", "SharePoint Upgrade", "Intranet - HR Compliance", "Lookup System", "CFS System", "SharePoint Advanced Search", and "Storage". Test-named channels in production Teams indicate workspaces created for experimentation that were never retired.

Without an owner responsible for each Team, there is no one to evaluate which channels are active and which can be deleted. Channels like "Intranet - HR Compliance" suggest sensitive topics being handled in a workspace that has no owner.

PASS No SharePoint sites are classified as inactive

All 99 SharePoint sites in scope returned no inactive classification. The scan examined sites across both the main and personal SharePoint domains.

This is a positive signal. It does not override the need for a group expiration policy — inactivity classification and expiration are separate controls — but it confirms that sites are not dormant at the storage level based on the telemetry recorded.

External Access & Guest Governance
NO-GO Guest invitations are unrestricted — any licensed user can invite external guests

The B2B collaboration setting is configured to allow guest invitations from everyone, with no restriction. Any licensed user in the tenant can invite an external guest account at will, without approval or oversight.

Combined with the absence of access reviews (below), this means external accounts enter the tenant without approval and remain indefinitely without review. The 24 guest accounts currently in the directory were admitted under this open configuration. Restricting guest invitation rights to administrators or designated approvers is the minimum change needed before any access review program is meaningful.

NO-GO No access review exists for the 24 guest accounts in the tenant

There are 24 guest accounts in the directory. Zero access reviews have been configured for any of them. No review scope, no review schedule, and no review history exists for guest membership.

Access reviews exist to answer the question: does this external person still need access? Without them, guest accounts persist until someone manually removes them, which in practice means they stay indefinitely. The 24 accounts currently in the tenant have never been certified as still needed by any responsible party. This is an Entra ID Governance control — configuring recurring access reviews for all guest accounts is the remediation.

PASS None of the 24 guest accounts are flagged as inactive based on sign-in activity

The staleness scan across all 24 guest accounts returned a stale count of zero. No guest account was identified as dormant based on sign-in activity recorded at the time of the scan.

This does not substitute for a formal access review — an account can be signing in and still represent an access grant that is no longer appropriate. But it does confirm that the 24 guests are active, not abandoned dormant accounts.

PASS Cross-tenant access policy is configured

The tenant has a cross-tenant access policy in place. This governs how users from other Entra ID tenants are handled at the trust boundary level, separate from the B2B guest invitation setting.

The presence of this policy is the correct baseline. Its configuration details were not returned in the telemetry scope of this report; the access review and invitation restriction gaps above remain the priority regardless.

Sensitivity Labels & Site Classification
PASS All 99 SharePoint sites carry a sensitivity label — full coverage

Every one of the 99 sites in scope has a sensitivity label applied. The scan confirmed 99 sites with a label out of 99 sites examined, across both the main SharePoint tenant and personal site collections.

This is the strongest governance signal in the tenant. Consistent label application means classification-based controls — data loss prevention rules, conditional access policies, sharing restrictions — have a surface to operate against. The gap is that the groups and Teams associated with these sites largely have no owner to maintain that label classification as content and membership evolve.

Policy Enforcement & Teams Structure
PASS All 18 Teams have a meeting policy and a messaging policy assigned

Every Team in the inventory — all 18 — has both a meeting policy and a messaging policy assigned. Policy coverage is complete at the Teams level for these two policy types.

Policy assignment confirms the administrative scaffolding is in place. The concern is that these policies are applied to Teams that have no owner — meaning there is no one accountable for verifying that the assigned policies remain appropriate as the Team's purpose or membership changes.

CONDITIONAL One of the 18 Teams is set to Public visibility — Test NASA

17 of the 18 Teams are Private. One — Test NASA — is configured as Public, meaning any user in the tenant can join it without an invitation or approval. The remaining 17 Teams are Private.

A Team named "Test NASA" being the only public Team suggests this was created for testing and not reviewed. Public Teams are appropriate only when open, self-service participation is the intent. With no owner assigned, there is no one to change its visibility or evaluate whether it should remain public.

CONDITIONAL All 18 Teams have guest members, with no access reviews in place

Every one of the 18 Teams has at least one guest member. Guest settings data was recorded for all 18. Combined with the absence of guest access reviews and the absence of Team owners, this means external participants are present in every Team workspace with no periodic certification of whether their access is still warranted.

Remediating Team ownership first creates the accountable party who can then participate in or respond to access reviews. Configuring access reviews without owners to action them produces incomplete reviews.

Change Awareness — Message Center
CONDITIONAL 108 major service-change notices are open in the Message Center

The Message Center holds 440 items in total. Of these, 108 are categorized as "Plan for change" — notices from Microsoft that require the tenant administrator to plan a response before a deadline, as a feature will change, be removed, or require action to maintain current behaviour. A further set are categorized as "Prevent or fix issue" — notices where inaction results in a service degradation or broken workflow. The remainder are categorized as "Stay informed."

Governance-relevant items visible in the title list include: retirement of Exchange Web Services, retirement of SharePoint Alerts, changes to SharePoint Designer 2013 support, OneDrive storage quota enforcement for unlicensed accounts, changes to Teams Live Events, and changes to EWS-based personal Bookings controls. Each "Plan for change" item has a deadline. Unreviewed, they become missed deadlines. A tenant with no formal Message Center review process treats 108 mandatory-response items as background noise. Assign ownership of Message Center review to a named administrator on a regular cadence.
