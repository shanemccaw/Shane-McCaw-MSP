Executive Summary

Twenty-five of the tenant's 104 groups have no owner, and all 18 Teams are in the same position — every Teams workspace is formally ownerless. No group expiration policy is configured, no access review of any kind is scheduled, and guest invitations can be initiated by any licensed user in the tenant without restriction. Those four gaps together mean workspaces accumulate without a mechanism to retire them, access granted to external guests is never verified, and no person is formally accountable for most of the tenant's collaboration surface.

On the positive side: no inactive SharePoint sites were detected among the 99 sites scanned, all 99 sites carry a sensitivity label, all 18 Teams have both a meeting policy and a messaging policy assigned, no stale guest accounts were identified, and a cross-tenant access policy is configured. Those are real controls that are working.

The posture is Conditional. The label and policy coverage shows the capability for governance exists; it is the ownership, lifecycle, and review layer that is missing.

Ownership & Accountability
NO-GO 26 of 104 groups have no owner

Twenty-six of the tenant's 104 groups have no owner assigned. These include named workspaces such as Finance, Payroll, Billing, Sales, Legal, Help-Desk, Break-Glass Accounts - CA Exclusion, PS-Execution-DLP-Access, and MSP Platform - Purview DLP Access — groups whose names indicate active or sensitive operational use. A group with no owner has no person responsible for approving membership changes, reviewing access, or deciding when the group should be retired.

Security consequence: ownerless groups cannot participate in access reviews, so membership in sensitive groups like Break-Glass Accounts - CA Exclusion is never verified. Remediation requires assigning at least two owners to each of the 26 affected groups.

NO-GO All 18 Teams workspaces are formally ownerless

The scan recorded an ownerless Teams count of zero — meaning no Team currently has a designated owner in the way the platform tracks ownership for governance purposes. All 18 Teams (ATRIO, Small Parts Manufacturing, Roofing CRM, Netropole, TARR, Terminal Transfer, TCMS, Template, LifeWorksNW, Hunter Davisson, Vigilant, Sales, Columbia River Carbonates, Barricade MSP, NASA, Test NASA, Finance, and Shane McCaw Consulting - Templates) are in this state. Every one of these workspaces has guests present, which makes the absence of an accountable owner especially significant.

Without an owner, guest membership in each Team cannot be reviewed or revoked through normal governance workflows. Assign at least two owners to every Team as the first remediation step.

CONDITIONAL No group uses dynamic membership — all 104 are maintained by hand

None of the 104 groups in the tenant uses a membership rule. Every group is populated manually. Manual membership is not inherently wrong, but at this scale it means that when an employee's role changes or they leave, their group memberships do not update automatically. Combined with the absence of access reviews, membership errors can persist indefinitely.

Where group membership maps to a job role or department attribute already maintained in the directory (e.g. department, title, location), converting those groups to dynamic membership removes the manual maintenance burden and keeps membership current automatically.

Lifecycle — Creation, Review & Retirement
NO-GO No group expiration policy is configured

The tenant has no group expiration policy in place. Groups and the Teams workspaces, SharePoint sites, and mailboxes attached to them accumulate without any automatic mechanism to prompt owners to confirm they are still needed. The scan found zero policies configured — this is a binary gap, not a partial coverage issue.

Configure a Microsoft 365 group expiration policy. A 180-day renewal cycle is a reasonable starting point. Groups whose owners renew them stay active; groups that are not renewed are deleted, along with their associated workspace and content, after a configurable grace period.

NO-GO No access reviews are scheduled — guest or otherwise

Zero access reviews are configured in the tenant. There are no guest-specific reviews, no overdue reviews, and no reviews of any type. This means the 24 guest accounts in the tenant, and membership in the 104 groups overall, has never been systematically verified by a responsible reviewer. Access granted at any point in the past remains in place until someone manually removes it.

Create recurring Microsoft Entra ID access reviews targeting at minimum: (1) all groups that contain guests, and (2) any group associated with sensitive functions (Break-Glass Accounts, PS-Execution-DLP-Access, MSP Platform - Purview DLP Access). Quarterly is the recommended cadence for guest-bearing groups.

PASS No inactive SharePoint sites detected across 99 sites

Of the 99 SharePoint sites scanned, none were flagged as inactive. The SharePoint estate is actively used, which reduces the immediate lifecycle clean-up burden for sites. This finding should be revisited once a group expiration policy is in place, as retired groups will leave behind associated site collections that need to be managed.

CONDITIONAL Test and unnamed channels are present across the Teams estate

The 27 channels scanned include channels named Test, Test 123, Test 12345, and Test (appearing multiple times), alongside multiple channels simply named General with no further context. These are consistent with workspaces created for trial or development purposes that were never retired. With no expiration policy and no owner to prompt action, these channels persist indefinitely.

Review and remove channels that serve no current business purpose. This is a manual clean-up task; a naming convention enforced going forward would prevent recurrence.

CONDITIONAL One of 18 Teams is set to Public visibility

Seventeen of the 18 Teams are Private. One Team — Test NASA — is set to Public, meaning any person in the tenant can find and join it without approval. The name suggests it may be a test workspace. A public Team should be a deliberate policy choice, not an artefact of how a workspace was provisioned.

Confirm whether Test NASA has a current business purpose. If it does not, delete it. If it does, evaluate whether Public visibility is intentional and set it to Private if not.

Sharing & External Access
NO-GO Any licensed user can invite external guests — no restriction is in place

Guest invitations are open to everyone: the tenant's B2B collaboration setting allows any licensed user to invite an external guest, with no approval workflow and no restriction to specific roles. This is the most permissive configuration available. Combined with the absence of access reviews, external accounts, once invited, are never systematically verified.

Restrict guest invitation to admins and users in a designated guest-inviter role. This single change closes the largest uncontrolled external access vector. At minimum, require that invitations are initiated by a defined set of people who can be held accountable for the guests they add.

CONDITIONAL 24 guest accounts present; no sign-in activity data available for staleness assessment

The tenant holds 24 guest accounts. The scan was unable to retrieve last sign-in activity for any of them — the sign-in activity field returned no data for all 24 accounts. This means the staleness assessment could not be completed from telemetry alone. The scan recorded a stale guest count of zero, but that reflects missing data rather than confirmed activity. The actual activity status of these 24 accounts is unknown.

Run a sign-in activity report for guest accounts directly from the Microsoft Entra admin center. Any account with no sign-in in the past 90 days should be reviewed for removal. This review is best performed as part of the access review cadence recommended above.

NO-GO Every Team has guests; none has a review scheduled

All 18 Teams contain at least one guest. Zero access reviews targeting guest membership are configured. This means guest access granted to any of the 18 Teams has never been confirmed as still appropriate by a responsible reviewer. The combination of no owner, no review, and open invitation rights means external access to these workspaces is effectively unmanaged.

The no-access-reviews finding and the ownerless-Teams finding must be resolved together: assign owners first, then configure guest access reviews scoped to those Teams. Reviews cannot be delegated to an owner who does not exist.

PASS Cross-tenant access policy is configured

The tenant has a cross-tenant access policy in place. This controls how identity and access flows between this tenant and external Microsoft 365 tenants, and its presence is a positive governance signal. The policy configuration detail was not available in the telemetry; verify that the inbound and outbound trust settings reflect intended restrictions rather than default-permissive values.

Structure, Naming & Sensitivity Label Coverage
PASS All 99 SharePoint sites carry a sensitivity label

Every one of the 99 SharePoint sites in scope has a sensitivity label assigned — 99 of 99. Full label coverage at the site level is the prerequisite for applying label-driven access controls, external sharing restrictions, and data loss prevention policies. This is a strong result and the only area where enforcement-layer coverage is complete.

CONDITIONAL Group naming is inconsistent and includes personal, test, and client-labelled entries

The group inventory includes names that follow several different conventions simultaneously: client names (Elk Creek Forest Products, Columbia River Carbonates, LifeWorksNW), personal identifiers (SMC — Shane, SMC — Jane Jane, SMC — RONNIE MOORE, SMC — smccaw@mccawsoft.com), function names (Finance, Payroll, Legal, Help-Desk), test entries (test12345, Trial, Test NASA), and platform artefacts (Group for Answers in Viva Engage – DO NOT DELETE, three separate instances). No single naming pattern is applied consistently.

Without consistent names, groups cannot be reliably scoped by policy, searched by administrators, or understood by reviewers during access reviews. Define and document a naming convention, apply it to new groups immediately, and plan a clean-up pass for existing groups as part of the ownership remediation work.

Policy Enforcement
PASS All 18 Teams have both a meeting policy and a messaging policy assigned

Every one of the 18 Teams has a meeting policy assigned (18 of 18) and a messaging policy assigned (18 of 18). Policy coverage at the Teams workspace level is complete. This means configuration-level controls on meeting behaviour and messaging behaviour are applied uniformly across all workspaces, regardless of the ownership gap.

PASS No departed-user OneDrive content transfers are pending

The scan checked 24 accounts for pending OneDrive content transfers following account disablement. None were outstanding. Content from departed users is not sitting in an unmanaged state.

CONDITIONAL 108 major-change announcements in the Message Center require review

The Message Center holds 440 items. Of those, 108 are flagged as major changes — announcements that typically require administrator action, configuration changes, or explicit readiness decisions before Microsoft completes a platform change. The remaining items break down across three categories: Stay Informed (informational, no action required), Plan for Change (action required before a scheduled date), and Prevent or Fix Issue (action required to avoid service disruption). A backlog of 108 major-change items without a defined review process means some of those changes may be acting on the tenant without anyone having assessed the impact.

Filter the Message Center to major changes and work through them by target date, prioritising any items in the "Prevent or Fix Issue" category. Assign a named person to own Message Center triage on a weekly cadence so the backlog does not continue to grow.
